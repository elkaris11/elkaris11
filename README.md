## My Mission

I'm using Microsoft Copilot and other AI tools to build accessibility systems for people with executive function challenges caused by neurodivergence, chronic illness, cognitive fatigue, and brain fog.

Many of the systems in this repository ecosystem started as solutions for problems I experience myself.

Living with lupus means some days come with brain fog, reduced mental bandwidth, memory issues, task-switching difficulties, and unpredictable energy levels. Tasks that look simple on paper can become significantly harder when attention, working memory, and cognitive endurance fluctuate throughout the day.

Rather than fighting that reality, I've started building systems around it.

My goal is not to become more productive for productivity's sake.

My goal is to reduce cognitive load.

## Why I'm Building These Projects

One of the biggest challenges I face is the gap between:

- Knowing what needs to be done
- Remembering it at the right time
- Organizing it into actionable steps
- Starting the task
- Staying focused long enough to finish it

Many people with executive function challenges know this gap well.

Because of that, many of my projects focus on:

- Cognitive load reduction
- Accessibility tooling
- Automation
- Decision support
- Structured workflows
- Information organization
- AI-assisted task guidance
- Executive function support systems

If a computer can remember something for me, organize something for me, categorize something for me, or guide me through it step-by-step, that's mental energy I no longer have to spend.

## The Executive Function Wake-Up Routine

One recurring challenge that inspired several of my projects is what I call the **Executive Function Startup Sequence**.

For many people, waking up is not the difficult part.

**Starting the day is.**

The brain immediately begins processing a long chain of questions:

- What day is it?
- What appointments do I have?
- What tasks are actually important today?
- What medications need to be taken?
- Which unfinished tasks still matter?
- What can safely wait?
- How much energy do I realistically have available?
- What is the next thing I should do?

Every decision carries a mental cost.

When enough of those decisions accumulate, decision fatigue can arrive before the day has meaningfully begun.

Several of my projects are attempts to reduce that startup friction through automation, reminders, workflow guidance, and AI-assisted decision support.

## How I Build Software

My workflow typically looks like this:

1. Identify a real-world problem
2. Define requirements and desired outcomes
3. Have Copilot generate an initial implementation
4. Test the implementation
5. Identify failures, bugs, and edge cases
6. Refine requirements
7. Iterate until the system solves the problem

This approach allows me to focus on:

- Problem solving
- System design
- Accessibility requirements
- Real-world usability
- Testing and validation

instead of spending all of my cognitive energy writing syntax by hand.

## Project Philosophy

I believe some of the best accessibility solutions come from lived experience.

People who struggle with a problem every day often understand it differently than people observing it from the outside.

The systems I build are experiments in creating tools that work with real human limitations instead of pretending those limitations do not exist.

If a workflow only works for a person with unlimited energy, perfect memory, and uninterrupted focus, then it probably is not accessible enough.
